In particular the nonprimitive incidence argument is **already proved**, not merely conjectured or a primitive-degree specialization. ## Consequential scope ambiguity: connected rule graphs

All inspected actual §3.1 consumers explicitly assume `rulesConnected : ∀ colour, (ruleSG system colour).Connected` (sometimes named `connected`); examples include `EIGSPointEvaluation.lean:20`, `EIGSRegularStronglyLocalDirichlet.lean:175` (declaration begins at 174), `EIGSFullMosco.lean:19`, and `EIGSOriginalMeasuredGHPSOfRules.lean:362`.

The manuscript's rule definition at **406–415** does not explicitly require connectedness or absence of isolated vertices. Canonicality at **590** quantifies over edges. It therefore does not, by itself, justify the sentence at **2865** that every internal vertex is electrically connected to the terminals. The convention at **585** says that a graph written as `G` in a general graph-theoretic setting is connected; its application to the directed rule graphs `R_i` is ambiguous.

Under the literal rule-definition/canonicality reading, there is a concrete counterexample: take the one-colour diamond with two paths of length two between terminals, orient its four edges as a directed four-cycle, and add one isolated internal vertex. Every edge lies on a simple terminal-to-terminal path; terminal distance is two; its oriented endpoint degree matrix is the strictly positive matrix with all four entries one, so degree primitivity survives. The extra vertex changes none of these conditions. Nevertheless the first substituted graph is disconnected, the ordinary graph metric is not finite, and the energy minimizer in **2849–2865** is nonunique because the isolated value is arbitrary. The canonical nested-vertex construction **2757–2765** is also compromised by the displayed edge-union substitution if isolated vertices are dropped later.

**Classification:** actual endpoint hypothesis mismatch unless the general connectedness convention is intended to cover every `R_i`. This is not an unconditional disproof under the latter interpretation. The clean repair is to say explicitly that the underlying undirected rule graphs are connected (equivalently, supplement edge canonicality with no isolated vertices in the present setting). Do not claim that edge canonicality alone proves vertex connectivity.

## New uniform-resolvent lemma: endpoints and precise residual scope

**Literal parameter/export gap:** the direct public uniform-resolvent endpoint at **EIGSNativeResolventUniformConvergence.lean:70–76** is restricted to `0 < step ≤ 1`. Under `step=1/λ`, it directly covers **λ ≥ 1**, whereas manuscript **3333** says every λ>0. I did not locate a direct exported all-λ result or a formal Laplace-integral bridge from the all-time uniform semigroup result. The all-λ mathematical conclusion follows routinely from semigroup convergence, the contraction bound and an exponentially weighted tail split, so this is **not an analytic obstruction or counterexample**. To certify the literal new lemma by a named Lean endpoint, add that bridge/export or cite the restricted resolvent plus all-time semigroup result with this qualification.

I did not locate a formal proof of the new gamma-time route. The endpoint is formally represented; a different valid proof is not a theorem-coverage gap.
